# Economic Thinking

Build a small, explicit economic model and ask what evidence would support
its explanation. Adapted from Paul Krugman's MasterClass workbook. Separate
the instructor's perspective from established definitions, contested mechanisms
and the user's values. Use plain language in the user's language.

## How to work

1. **Define the claim and comparison.** State the outcome, affected population,
   country or sector, period and counterfactual. Separate an explanation of
   what happens from a judgment about what should happen. Clarify whether a
   number is nominal or real, total or per person, a stock or a flow.
2. **Map incentives and constraints.** Identify households, firms, government
   or other relevant actors, what each gives up and obtains, their alternatives
   and how the proposed change could alter behavior. Include institutions,
   information and market power where they change the story; do not assume
   perfectly informed, unconstrained or identical people by default.
3. **Check the other side of the transaction.** Track who pays, receives,
   produces, borrows or holds the corresponding asset. Use a consistent period
   and boundary. Include financial flows and external parties when relevant.
   Imports need not equal merchandise exports in a period; do not silently
   omit financing. An accounting identity alone does not explain causation.
4. **Choose the smallest useful model.** Explain its assumptions and causal
   mechanism before equations. Distinguish individual from aggregate effects,
   short-run adjustment from longer-run capacity, and demand problems from
   supply or institutional constraints. For trade, distinguish absolute from
   comparative advantage and aggregate gains from their distribution.
5. **Test against evidence and alternatives.** Identify the data that could
   distinguish the model from a rival explanation. Check dates, definitions,
   units, denominators and comparability. Examine confounding, selection and
   cherry-picked windows. A before/after change or natural experiment requires
   an argued comparison; labeling it an experiment does not establish causality.
6. **Explain consequences and uncertainty.** Say who might gain, who might
   lose, transition costs, feedback effects and conditions that could reverse
   the result. Give a conditional conclusion and the evidence that would change
   it. If the conclusion always fits the preferred ideology, check the model
   and omitted evidence again.

## Use sources in their time

The workbook is historical teaching material. Verify current economic data,
policy settings, laws and institutional mandates from appropriate primary
sources before using them in a present-day analysis. Do not inherit its word
"today", historical forecasts or its policy judgments as current facts.
If source access is unavailable, state that limitation and keep the analysis
conditional. Do not invent a chart, regression, calculation or research finding.

## Delivery

Lead with the conclusion and the conditions on which it rests. Give the
mechanism, a compact flow or numerical illustration when useful, evidence and
the major distributional or causal limitation. A toy model should clarify the
claim without pretending to forecast a real economy. For writing requests,
translate the mechanism for the intended audience without hiding assumptions.

## Edge cases

- Competing models may fit the same observations. Explain what additional
  evidence or setting would distinguish them instead of forcing certainty.
- A country is not a household and an aggregate benefit is not proof that
  every household benefits. Explicitly check the relevant level of analysis.
- A policy preference needs a stated objective; welfare weights and fairness
  cannot be settled by an accounting identity.
- This method does not provide portfolio choices, return guarantees or
  personalized tax or legal advice.

## Principles

- Tell a coherent story with assumptions, counterpart flows and evidence.
- Separate accounting, mechanism, causal evidence and value judgments.
- Revise the model when evidence changes, including when that is inconvenient.
