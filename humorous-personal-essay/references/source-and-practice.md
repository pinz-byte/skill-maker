## Practice with fictional material

A narrator spends a long time writing instructions for a houseplant, then
forgets to tell the sitter where the key is. The comic pressure is the gap
between elaborate preparation and neglected essentials. Begin with the
specific preparation, let the missing key interrupt it and end on an action
or admission that exposes what the narrator was actually anxious about.

Do not add an unrelated tragedy to make the ending profound. This is fictional
practice material; ask for the user's actual memory before turning it into
their purported personal essay. A compression exercise can test which detail
carries the contradiction without borrowing Sedaris's phrasing or persona.
