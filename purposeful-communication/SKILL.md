# Purposeful Communication

Know what the audience needs answered, prepare thoroughly and listen closely
enough to depart from the script. Adapted from George Stephanopoulos's
MasterClass guide. Work in the user's language and adapt to the actual setting.

## How to work

1. **Define the purpose.** Identify the interviewee, audience, setting, time
   available and essential information. Distinguish learning, accountability,
   explanation and an empathetic personal account. Do not default to an
   adversarial tone when the goal calls for another approach.
2. **Prepare an evidence brief.** Review supplied material and, where needed,
   appropriate primary sources, past statements and alternative interpretations.
   Mark established facts, attributed claims and unknowns. Current events need
   current verification. Never invent a quotation or assume an allegation true.
3. **Prioritize essential questions.** Select the few questions that make the
   conversation worthwhile. Ask one thing at a time, using plain language.
   Start open when exploring, then use a direct closed question when a specific
   commitment, date or fact needs confirmation. Anticipate likely branches as
   preparation, not as predictions of actual answers.
4. **Set the conditions.** Clarify time, purpose, recording or attribution
   arrangements when relevant. Respect personal boundaries and accessibility.
   Prepare a fair factual challenge for accountability interviews, not a trap.
   A subject's refusal can be reported accurately without inventing a motive.
5. **Listen and follow the answer.** Respond to what was actually said. Clarify
   an unfamiliar term, request an example, pursue a new fact or return to an
   unanswered essential question. Use silence to allow thought, not to pressure
   a disclosure. Do not ask a bundle of questions that lets the essential one vanish.
6. **Close with a factual check.** Confirm names, dates, commitments and
   remaining uncertainty. Distinguish the source's account from independently
   verified facts. Identify what needs checking after the conversation and
   preserve the meaning and context of any quotation.

## Communicating during uncertainty

Lead with what is known, who supports it and what remains unconfirmed. Separate
observation, attribution and interpretation. Be clear and humane without filling
gaps with confident speculation. Explain corrections plainly when facts change.
For an interview plan, include the question that could resolve the key unknown.

## Delivery

Provide the purpose, concise evidence brief, essential questions in a useful
order and adaptive follow-ups. For a supplied transcript, identify the strongest
missed follow-up with the actual passage that supports it. For a news update,
deliver the verified message and uncertainties rather than a full interview plan.
Label roleplay as fictional and do not report it as an interview conducted.

## Edge cases

- Nonverbal cues can prompt a neutral check-in. They do not prove deceit,
  discomfort or agreement. Do not apply communication percentages as a
  universal formula or infer thoughts from a silent video.
- A person describing trauma may decline details or pause. Do not force
  disclosure, use unwanted touch or imitate therapy to obtain a quote.
- Accountability can be firm: contrast a documented action with a documented
  promise, ask the direct question and seek the missing answer respectfully.
- Research and drafting do not authorize contacting sources, recording,
  sending questions or publishing an interview.

## Principles

- Prepare deeply; ask simply; follow the actual answer.
- Seek information rather than perform cleverness or manufacture a confession.
- Keep the audience's understanding and the source's actual words intact.
