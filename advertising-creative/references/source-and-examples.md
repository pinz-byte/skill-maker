## Historical teaching cases

The following summarize the guide's account; they are not current market
research or independently measured campaign results.

- **Milk, pp. 13-14:** a research observation about noticing milk when it is
  missing became an absence-centered campaign. The transferable move is to
  find the concrete moment of need rather than repeat a familiar benefit.
- **Chevys, p. 12:** daily food freshness was expressed through ads made for
  that day. The production method carried the proposition. Reusing this move
  requires a genuine product truth and a feasible production schedule.
- **Nike skateboarding, p. 16:** other athletes were depicted receiving the
  treatment skateboarders encountered. Reversing roles exposed the underlying
  tension rather than merely declaring sympathy for the audience.
- **Logitech, p. 28:** a cultural association was developed into a character
  situation with an unexpected casting choice. The lesson is to develop an
  association into a scene, not assume a celebrity appearance is an idea.

## Worked application: hypothetical repair service

**Fictional brief:** a neighborhood bike workshop wants more bookings. The
brief states that it gives a written estimate before starting work. The budget
supports a short phone-shot video, not a celebrity or elaborate set.

**Hypothesized insight:** some riders delay repairs because they fear agreeing
to an unknown bill. This needs customer evidence; do not call it a research finding.

**Idea:** make the price visible before the repair becomes visible.

**Execution:** the story appears to start with an elaborate repair sequence,
then a hand stops it and places the estimate in view. Only after the customer
agrees does the repair begin. The workshop's actual process enables the payoff.
Write the language in the workshop's real voice and include a booking action
if immediate response is the objective.

**Estimated 15-second beat map:** 0-3 seconds establish the damaged bike;
3-6 interrupt the impending work; 6-10 show and explain the estimate;
10-13 show agreement and the repair beginning; 13-15 identify the workshop
and booking action. Total: 15 seconds. Verify readability and speech timing
with a rough cut before treating the duration as confirmed.

**Test:** show a rough cut to intended customers and ask what happened before
the repair, which workshop offered it, and what they would do next. Misreading
the offer as free repair would require revising the execution. Recalling the
scene without the workshop would require improving brand attribution. This
checks comprehension, not sales lift; response needs a separate measurement.
