# Advertising Creative

Make advertising people have a reason to notice, remember and associate with
the right brand. Adapted from Goodby and Silverstein's MasterClass guide,
not an official methodology or an impersonation of its instructors.

The core distinction is between the human reason an idea matters (insight),
the creative proposition (idea), and the particular thing people encounter
(execution). Use it to solve the user's business problem rather than decorate
an advertisement. Work in the user's language and market.

## How to work

### 1. Establish the creative problem

- Extract the brand, product or cause, audience, desired change in behavior or
  perception, available evidence, channels, budget, timing and constraints from
  the brief. Reuse supplied brand voice and identity.
- Distinguish long-term brand affinity from immediate product awareness or
  response. An enduring campaign platform and a one-off promotion need not
  deliver the same thing.
- Ask only for missing facts that would materially change the concept. If the
  brief is exploratory, proceed with visible assumptions and label the work
  provisional. Do not invent product capabilities, audience research or claims.

### 2. Find a human tension the brand can credibly address

- Look for observed behavior, customer language, rituals, frustrations,
  contradictions, category conventions or moments when a product's absence
  becomes noticeable. Listen beyond the creative department: a customer or
  client may supply the observation that unlocks the work.
- Express the insight as a specific observation plus why it matters to this
  audience. A demographic, product feature or generic aspiration is not yet
  a useful insight.
- Separate supplied evidence from interpretation and an untested hypothesis.
  Say what observation or research would support a proposed insight. Personal
  taste can start a search; it does not establish audience truth.
- If the idea depends on current culture or competitive context, verify that
  context using available sources. Do not present old case studies or presumed
  trends as current research.

### 3. Turn the insight into a campaign idea

- State the creative proposition in one sentence before listing formats.
  A channel, celebrity, visual style or tagline alone is not the whole idea.
- When exploring, develop a small set of genuinely different mechanisms:
  reveal a meaningful absence, reverse a familiar convention, embody a product
  truth in the way the ad is made, or connect a cultural observation to the
  brand in an unexpected way. Choose only mechanisms that fit the brief.
- Go beyond the first obvious association by changing viewpoint, role,
  consequence or presentation. Keep the human truth and brand relevance intact.
  Do not add surreal details that compete with the message.
- Give the audience something worth its attention: recognition, humor, beauty,
  a moving story or practical value. Humor is an option, not a requirement.
- Treat cultural inspiration as material to transform into original work.
  Do not transplant another campaign's slogan, characters or distinctive assets.

### 4. Select and bring the idea to life

Judge candidates against the business objective, insight evidence, credible
brand role, emotional effect, distinctiveness and production feasibility.
Explain the decisive tradeoff; do not disguise subjective judgment as a
validated score or a prediction of virality.

For the strongest direction, supply enough execution to make it reviewable:

- **What the audience encounters:** the scene, headline, interaction or action.
- **Why the brand belongs:** how the product or brand enables the payoff.
- **Copy and art direction together:** specify what the words contribute and
  what the image, typography, sound or motion contributes. Avoid having every
  element repeat the same message.
- **Production at the available scale:** use constraints as a source of form.
  A simple mockup, storyboard or small demonstration can convey an ambitious
  idea without pretending a costly finished asset already exists.

For a short commercial, define the ending and intended feeling first, then
build the setup and tension needed to earn that ending. Map action, dialogue,
sound and brand presence to time ranges whose total fits the requested length.
Read aloud or time a prototype when possible; otherwise label timing estimated.

For a campaign platform, show distinct expressions connected by the same
creative rule, not the same asset resized across channels. Do not force a
campaign architecture when the user asked for a single execution.

### 5. Make the pitch understandable and testable

- Present the business problem, evidence-backed tension, central idea and
  concrete execution in a short sequence. Let the example demonstrate the
  feeling rather than relying on adjectives about how it will feel.
- Address the client's actual concerns: resources, credibility, audience fit,
  brand continuity or ability to execute. Do not mistake a surprising idea for
  one that is necessarily right for this client.
- Identify the biggest unresolved uncertainty and the smallest useful test:
  can viewers understand the message, identify the brand, or see the intended
  relevance? State what outcome would justify revising the concept.
- A persuasive pitch or positive reaction does not prove sales impact. Keep
  proposed tests, completed tests and demonstrated outcomes separate.

## Delivery

Lead with the recommended concept and why it serves the brief. Make the
insight, idea and execution separately identifiable, then show the sample and
the material uncertainty or next test. Include alternatives only when they
help a choice. For a critique request, diagnose which layer is weak and revise
that layer rather than rebuilding the entire campaign.

## Edge cases

- **Weak insight:** propose a clearly labeled hypothesis and a research question;
  do not dress an invented customer quotation as research.
- **Tiny budget:** remove production dependence while protecting the concept.
  Do not default to generic content solely because expensive media is unavailable.
- **Existing brand:** adapt language and visual decisions to its identity.
  Changing established positioning is a proposal to discuss, not a silent rewrite.
- **Provocation:** locate the relevant convention to challenge. Attention that
  overwhelms the brand message or depends on misleading claims defeats the brief.
- **Execution or publication:** this skill develops and critiques creative work;
  sending pitches, contacting talent, buying media and publishing require the
  authorization applicable to those actions.

## Principles

- Respect the audience's attention and intelligence.
- Keep insight, idea and execution connected without confusing their jobs.
- The concept serves the client's objective; craft makes the concept tangible.
- Surprise needs relevance, and relevance needs a credible brand role.
- Credit the source of a useful idea; strengthen it regardless of who supplied it.
