# Ideas Into Art

Find the essential gesture in source material and develop a tangible way for
an audience to experience it. Adapted from Es Devlin's MasterClass guide.
Preserve the user's artistic voice rather than reproduce Devlin's aesthetic.

## How to work

1. **Absorb the source.** Read, listen or inspect the material actually supplied.
   Identify its movement, emotional tension, images and turning points. Separate
   direct observations from interpretation. If the source is missing, ask for
   the relevant material instead of inventing its meaning.
2. **Find the essence.** State the experience the work should create and the
   common thread connecting its parts. Explore several visual or spatial
   translations and identify a central gesture. A cube is one artist's recurring
   motif, not a required solution. Seek a form that fits this work.
3. **Give the concept a direction.** If useful, draft a short creative manifesto
   naming the purpose, audience, commitments and boundaries of the work.
   Make it a guide for choices, not a grand declaration detached from them.
   Combine references by their relationships, not by copying signature designs.
4. **Externalize and vary.** Propose or make rough sketches that test composition,
   scale, transition, light, sound or movement. Capture a variation before
   polishing the first option. Explain what each sketch tests; a textual
   description is not an actual sketch or render.
5. **Move into space.** Develop a simple physical or digital model when depth
   changes the idea. State whether it is conceptual, proportional or built to a
   specified scale. Include a human or known-size reference. Explore viewpoints,
   sightlines, audience movement and how the work changes over time.
6. **Test the gesture.** Show the prototype to an appropriate viewer or propose
   that test. Ask what they perceived without first explaining the intended
   meaning. Compare it to the aim, then change the form while protecting the
   essential gesture. Do not report an unperformed audience test as evidence.

## Collaboration and material choices

Identify the skills the concept needs, who would resolve technical unknowns
and how changes will be communicated. Treat collaborators' constraints as
design information. Confidence in an idea does not override engineering judgment.

Plan for disassembly, reuse, sourcing, transport and waste at the concept stage.
Explain sustainability choices through actual material and lifecycle conditions;
do not assume a natural or recycled material is automatically lower impact.
For public or physical installations, flag accessibility and fabrication questions
that a prototype cannot settle. Do not present a render as structural validation.

## Delivery

Give the central gesture, relation to the source, intended audience experience,
prototype or clear prototype brief, and the next test. For a manifesto-only
request, deliver the manifesto. For an existing sketch, critique the translation
and propose the smallest informative revision.

## Edge cases

- No clear idea: collect varied references and make small experiments rather
  than waiting for inspiration. A break can help; it is not a scientific guarantee.
- Tight budget: preserve the gesture using simpler materials or reduced scope.
  Do not hide unbuilt mechanics behind a persuasive visualization.
- Adaptation: acknowledge source material and distinguish interpretation from
  quotation. Do not promise rights to use another artist's work.
- Making a plan does not authorize procurement, construction or publication.

## Principles

- The form should communicate a specific gesture, not just look impressive.
- Use sketches and models to discover, not only to sell a finished decision.
- Change materials and mechanics without losing the work's essential meaning.
