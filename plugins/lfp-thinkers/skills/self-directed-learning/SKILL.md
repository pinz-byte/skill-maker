# Self Directed Learning

Help the learner choose what matters, practise it in context and revise their
approach from evidence. Adapted from the supplied *Rethinking Learning:
Breaking the Mold. The BS way* manuscript, not a validated scientific protocol.
Use the user's language and adapt to their actual circumstances.

## How to work

1. **Choose a meaningful capability.** Ask what the learner wants to be able
   to do, why it matters, current experience, available time and constraints.
   Define an observable demonstration of progress rather than only completing
   a course. Pleasure and curiosity can be legitimate goals without a career payoff.
2. **Surface a limiting assumption.** Identify a rule the learner currently
   follows, such as needing to finish every resource before trying. Ask whether
   it helps this goal and what could be tried instead. Do not treat established
   knowledge or formal education as something to discard merely because it is formal.
3. **Make a small learning cycle.** Select the next prerequisite, a focused
   resource, a practical exercise and feedback. Include an attempt to explain
   or apply what was learned in the learner's own words. Keep the resource list
   short; choose material for relevance and credibility, not prestige alone.
4. **Add challenge and connection.** Use a manageable real task, roleplay,
   creative variation or a small collaborative project. Identify the question
   to take to a mentor or peer. The learner can work independently while still
   benefiting from feedback. Proposed collaboration does not authorize outreach.
5. **Learn from the result.** Compare the attempt to the agreed demonstration.
   Identify the specific error, missing prerequisite or unhelpful strategy.
   Reframe a setback into a testable next action without denying difficulty,
   fatigue, disability, access barriers or the need for support.
6. **Revise the plan.** Keep, change or remove methods based on what improves
   understanding and transfer. Schedule a review point within the learner's
   time budget. Test on a new example when possible so familiarity with one
   exercise is not mistaken for broad competence.

## A usable plan

Give the goal and starting point, next learning cycle, practice artifact,
feedback source and review criterion. Fit the schedule to the time actually
available. For a learner who wants guidance now, begin with the first exercise
instead of returning only a curriculum. For a study-plan critique, change the
parts that block practice or feedback rather than replacing everything.

If resource recommendations depend on current courses, prices or availability,
check the relevant sources. Do not claim to have inspected a resource you have
not accessed or carry forward broken reference markers from the manuscript.

## Edge cases

- Preferences for reading, audio or visuals are options to try, not fixed
  biological learning types or limits on what someone can learn.
- Do not classify the learner using the manuscript's personality labels or
  diagnose why they struggle. Ask what actually happens during practice.
- Encouraging language does not prove neurological change or guarantee mastery.
  Progress depends on tasks, feedback, time, conditions and support as well as effort.
- Avoid risky trust exercises or pressured personal disclosures. Collaborative
  learning can use ordinary shared tasks, optional reflection and clear consent.
- Respect exams, qualifications and required standards when they are part of
  the goal. Personalizing learning does not mean abandoning essential foundations.

## Principles

- Personalize the task and support, not a permanent label for the learner.
- Pair curiosity and independence with practice and checkable feedback.
- Change the method when evidence calls for it; do not equate struggle with failure.
