# Humorous Personal Essay

Turn a small observed moment into a personal essay with comic movement and
something at stake beyond the joke. Adapted from David Sedaris's MasterClass
guide. Preserve the user's own language and voice; do not reproduce the author's
signature style, phrases or recurring devices as an imitation.

## How to work

1. **Gather the actual material.** Identify a diary entry, memory, awkward
   exchange or telling detail. Ask what the user recalls and what remains
   uncertain. If no material exists, offer a short observation prompt or an
   explicitly fictional example; do not invent the user's life or exact dialogue.
2. **Find the larger pressure.** Ask what the incident reveals about the narrator's
   desire, embarrassment, contradiction or changed understanding. Connect it to
   a related event only when the connection earns its place. A funny anecdote
   does not become an essay simply by adding more description.
3. **Build a scene.** Choose details that reveal action and character. Let the
   narrator's assumptions encounter something that complicates them. Select
   dialogue for its function and preserve the distinction between remembered
   wording, paraphrase and an exact recorded quotation.
4. **Choose the comic mechanism.** Explore situational incongruity, clearly
   signaled exaggeration, genuinely funny quoted speech or self-directed irony.
   The narrator can be implicated in the absurdity without being humiliated.
   Do not turn a vulnerable bystander into a disposable punchline or force
   every sentence to perform a joke.
5. **Give the essay an ending.** Seek a final action, image, admission or callback
   that changes the meaning of what preceded it. Emotional force must be earned
   by the material; do not manufacture tragedy, a moral or a sentimental reveal.
   An open ending can work if it feels chosen rather than merely stopped.
6. **Rewrite for movement and sound.** Reorder before polishing. Cut a favorite
   joke that distorts the essay; compress setup, allow a pause where it helps,
   and remove explanation that repeats what the scene already delivers.
   Read aloud or review cadence explicitly. If a live reading is available,
   distinguish actual audience response from predicted laughter.

## Delivery

For drafting, deliver the essay or requested passage, followed by a short note
on the key revision and any factual gaps. For a critique, locate the weak turn,
unearned ending or stalled setup in the actual text and suggest a focused fix.
For practice, offer one bounded exercise such as reducing a piece to exactly
100 words; verify a requested word count rather than guessing it.

## Edge cases

- Nonfiction permits selection and reflection, not presenting invented events,
  composite people or exact quotes as verified fact. Label deliberate fictionalization.
- Writing about real people raises privacy and relationship considerations.
  Consider identifying details and sensitive disclosures. Do not promise a
  legal right to publish merely because an event is remembered.
- Public observation does not authorize intrusive recording or contacting
  subjects. Producing a draft does not authorize publication or sharing.
- Writing habits should fit the user's circumstances; prolific output is a
  practice to adapt, not a required daily quota or guarantee of publication.

## Principles

- Let an observation grow into meaning without sacrificing its specificity.
- Protect truth and the narrator's original voice while shaping the telling.
- A good joke serves the essay; an earned ending changes how it is remembered.
