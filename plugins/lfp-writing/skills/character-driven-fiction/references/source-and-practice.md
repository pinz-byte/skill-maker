## Fictional discovery exercise

A twelve-year-old is asked to bring an object representing their family to
school. Instead of describing them as embarrassed, write the moment they
choose what to pack and hide something else. Give them a concrete reason
for the choice that belongs to their experience, not an adult's explanation.

Add a conversation with a classmate who misunderstands the object. Let the
protagonist choose whether to correct the misunderstanding. Check what the
choice changes, what remains unsaid and whether the narrative slips into an
adult voice. The exercise can expose a story direction without prescribing
the child's identity, family structure or the story's moral.
