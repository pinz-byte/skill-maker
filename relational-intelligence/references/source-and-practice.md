This is an original synthesis, not a validated assessment, clinical protocol or
formal algorithm attributed to Perel. The sequence, outcome checks, privacy
rules and explicit coercion/safety exceptions are editorial choices. The guide's
historical and neuroscience sections are not used to claim clinical efficacy.
Do not generalize its suggestion of a richer voice channel into a rule against
written communication. Its touch suggestion is not a general-purpose technique;
physical contact is never necessary to use this skill.

## Optional relational reflection

Offer only when it serves the user's goal; no full history is required.
The user may reflect privately rather than disclose answers.

- What did you learn about disagreement, closeness and authority while growing up?
- What role do you repeatedly take in this relationship, and what does it cost?
- Which part of that role still serves you, and which would you like to change?
- What boundary needs more protection or more room in this particular context?
- What is one response you can choose differently without assuming the other
  person will change?

These questions invite reflection; answers do not prove the origin of a pattern.
For connection rather than conflict, try a voluntary story invitation such as:
"What experience changed how you work with other people?" Let the person pass
or keep the story light. Do not require personal disclosures at work.

## Worked example: interrupted in a meeting

**Fictional account:** the user reports being interrupted twice in a project
meeting and concludes that their colleague considers their work worthless.

**Separate the layers:** two reported interruptions are the observation; being
devalued is the user's interpretation; hurt and frustration are the experienced
impact. Respect and recognition are possible concerns to explore, not a motive
established about the colleague.

**Possible opening in Spanish:**

"En la reunion de hoy no pude terminar dos veces lo que estaba explicando.
Me frustro porque queria que se entendiera mi propuesta. En la proxima, te
pido dejarme terminar y despues revisamos las objeciones. Como lo viste tu?"

**Next check:** notice whether the colleague lets the user complete their
point in the next meeting. If interruptions recur, the next action may involve
meeting facilitation or an appropriate workplace process. Better wording alone
cannot solve a structural power problem. Adapt to any retaliation risk.

## Worked example: taking a pause without disappearing

**Fictional account:** in a mutually willing, nonthreatening relationship, one
person wants to settle an argument immediately while the other needs a break.

**Possible wording:**

"Quiero hablar de esto, pero ahora me cuesta escuchar sin defenderme.
Necesito una pausa. Te parece que retomemos hoy a las siete y hablemos solo
de lo que paso esta manana?"

The return time is a proposal, not a unilateral demand. If agreed, following
through provides observable information about reliability. Pursuing someone
through a room to force a conversation would intensify the pattern. If there
is intimidation or danger, do not treat this as an ordinary reciprocal conflict.

## Worked example: a limited trust experiment

**Fictional account:** a friend repeatedly failed to return borrowed objects.
The user wants to preserve the friendship while no longer lending expensive items.

**Boundary:** "Quiero seguir viendote, pero por ahora no voy a prestar mis
herramientas. Podemos hacer el arreglo juntos en mi casa si te sirve."

If the user later wants to test reliability, they can choose an inexpensive,
replaceable item and agree on a return date. They need not do so. A successful
return supports only that limited observation; it does not prove global trust.
The other person's disappointment does not invalidate the user's limit.
