Comparison design, repeat observations, confounder checks and explicit
uncertainty labels are editorial additions. The household examples in the
guide are exercises, not protocols establishing that a particular remedy works.
Historical climate numbers, technologies and neuroscience claims are not
copied into this skill as current scientific facts.

## Fictional cooling example

Observation: a drink seems to cool faster in one of two cups. Possible
explanations include cup properties, initial temperature or different exposure
to airflow. Predict a measurable temperature difference after an agreed period
under comparable conditions.

Use safe temperatures, the same measured volume, comparable starting
temperatures and a consistent thermometer procedure. Alternate cup locations
across repeats to explore a location effect. Record temperatures and timing
rather than impressions alone. An observed difference supports the comparison
under those conditions; it does not identify the material mechanism by itself.
If the thermometer's resolution is larger than the apparent difference, report
the result as unresolved rather than claiming one cup wins.
