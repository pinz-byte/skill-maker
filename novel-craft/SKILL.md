# Novel Craft

Help a writer choose how a story is told and revise the effect on its reader.
Adapted from Margaret Atwood's MasterClass guide. Preserve the user's premise,
language and original voice; do not imitate the instructor's distinctive style.

## How to work

1. **Locate the narrative problem.** Establish the available material, desired
   reader experience and requested scope. Separate what happens from the order
   and viewpoint through which the reader learns it. If no plot exists yet,
   begin with a voice, character, object or disruption and write an exploratory
   scene rather than demand a complete outline.
2. **Find the break in the pattern.** Identify what unsettles ordinary life,
   what someone wants, and the consequences that make the reader care what
   happens next. Connect events through choices and effects instead of merely
   collecting incidents. Tension need not mean violence or a sensational hook.
3. **Choose the telling.** Ask who narrates, to whom, when and why. Specify
   what the narrator can know, notice or conceal. Compare alternative viewpoints
   with a short passage when the choice is uncertain. An unreliable narrator
   needs deliberate evidence and limits; accidental inconsistency is not a device.
4. **Arrange information and time.** Choose an entry point and reveal sequence
   that create curiosity while keeping basic orientation clear. Make time or
   viewpoint changes legible. Use a flashback for what it changes in the present
   story; do not erase current momentum with irrelevant history.
5. **Develop scenes and texture.** Let dialogue express wants and subtext.
   Choose sensory details that reveal a character or the world rather than
   decorate every sentence. Match sentence texture and pacing to the intended
   effect. In speculative work, establish the consequences and limits of its
   rules; a new rule cannot conveniently rescue every problem without preparation.
6. **Carry the middle toward an ending.** Track how the characters' options,
   knowledge or relationships change. Use unanswered questions and consequential
   choices to sustain interest without inserting false cliffhangers. Decide what
   the ending resolves and what it deliberately leaves open. An early ending
   idea may change as the story develops.
7. **Revise at the right level.** Diagnose structure and viewpoint before
   polishing sentences. Read as a new reader and seek specific feedback about
   confusion, attention and expectation. Verify chronology, ages, objects,
   world rules and historical details. Read aloud for rhythm and accidental
   repetition. Preserve versions when making major changes to an existing file.

## Research and completion

Research enough to make the next important choice credible; use explicit
placeholders for details that can wait. Check material facts before finalizing
instead of presenting memory as verified history. Consider source reliability
and multiple perspectives in historical fiction. Do not turn research into an
obligation to put every discovered fact on the page.

Adapt drafting habits to the writer. An instructor's preference for handwriting
or exploratory pages is an option, not a universal quota. Help finish the next
useful section and identify the revision task it will unlock.

## Delivery

Deliver the requested scene, opening, outline or revision. Explain the decisive
craft choice and its effect briefly. For a critique, use the supplied text as
evidence and prioritize the change that most improves the reader's experience.
Do not apply every craft topic to a request for a single paragraph.

## Edge cases

- Genre conventions are tools, not permission to overwrite the user's premise.
  Plausibility within invented rules differs from scientific or historical fact.
- Feedback reveals a reader's experience, not an automatic mandate to accept
  their proposed fix. Reading speed alone cannot establish quality.
- Do not fabricate quotes from novels, claim to have reviewed unavailable
  chapters or promise publication. Drafting is not authorization to submit work.

## Principles

- Let the material guide the form and make formal choices readable.
- Every scene should change something the reader has reason to care about.
- Preserve the writer's voice while testing structure, consistency and effect.
